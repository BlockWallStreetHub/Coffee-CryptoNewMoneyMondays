What happened

France: 90 attacks on Bitcoiners in 7 months

Security Self-custody

Cointelegraph Magazine reports France is now the world's most dangerous place for crypto holders. The French Interior Ministry logged 90 crypto-related cases (kidnapping, abduction, extortion, threats, violent theft) from Jan 1 to mid-August 2026, about one every 2.5 days. The ministry only began recording these on Jan 1, 2026, so there is no 2025 comparison.

Why it's happening

  • Chainalysis' Eric Jardine says the surge is "very likely" tied to a significant data breach. A 2024 French tax-authority leak (a Paris-area official) may have started it; attacks surged from late Nov 2024.
  • A January 2026 breach at crypto tax firm Waltio exposed about 50,000 users.
  • Attackers do recon through social media, blockchain analysis, leaked data and insider tips.
  • Bull Bitcoin's Francis Pouliot argues the EU's DAC8 reporting rules raise the risk for holders.

BWS takeaway: the weak link isn't your hardware wallet, it's who knows you hold

The article's point: seed phrases and hardware wallets don't help if attackers already know who you are and where you live. Practical privacy habits to talk through on the show:

  • Don't advertise. No holdings, gains or wallet screenshots on social media. Treat "I'm a Bitcoiner" as private information.
  • Know who holds your data. Exchanges, tax software and filings are breach targets. Share the minimum, and know what each service has on you.
  • Keep the home address out of it. Use a P.O. box or business address where you can; separate crypto-related accounts from your home details.
  • Keep the "everything" out of one place. Spread access (multisig, time-locks, and trusted co-signers in different locations) so no single person can be forced to drain it.
  • Keep your inner circle small. Relatives and acquaintances are over 40% of cases in France.
  1. Does KYC and tax reporting make holders safer or more exposed?
  2. What's your personal privacy rule that you haven't posted online?
